\begin{abstract}
The \forget{} mutation simulates human users' memory failures within security ceremonies: when a value is forgotten, it becomes unavailable for constructing a message, and this lapse manifests as a \replace{}, \skipm{} or \neglect{} mutation that a standard verifier processes without alteration. The verifier's judgment rests on a syntactic examination of derivation possibilities, an approach that cannot capture the loss of a skill or the recovery triggered by a cue, and it offers no justification for its outcome.

We propose a reasoning framework for \forget{} based on the ambiguity-blocking, team-defeat variant of Defeasible Logic, DL($\partial$). For each step in a human interaction, the cognitive state is translated into a logical theory where owning a piece of information and being able to use it are distinct concepts. Every potential derivation of a candidate message is treated as a labeled conclusion. Failures to recall values, rules of inference, or entire derivations act as defeaters. Cues function as prioritized exceptions that can override a lapse without erasing it. Verdicts are determined by inspecting proof tags, and the proofs themselves serve as explanations.

We describe the conclusions that these compiled theories can reach and identify which proof-theoretic settings influence them: recovering from multiple lapses necessitates team defeat, whereas ambiguity blocking and propagation produce consistent results. This framework is a conservative extension of the syntactic test and can differentiate between states that the original could not. Its conclusions remain sound under bounded exploration, and determining usability can be done in polynomial time provided no complete derivation has been forgotten. An implementation in \XMen~2.0 matches an independent encoding of DL($\partial$), revealing a flaw in team defeat through this comparison, and its findings alter verdicts produced by Tamarin. An initial exploration of adversarial cues identifies situations where conflicts become mutual and the selected semantics determines whether a confused human proceeds or refrains from action.

\end{abstract}
