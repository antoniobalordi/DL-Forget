\begin{abstract}
The \forget{} mutation models memory lapses of human users in security ceremonies: a forgotten value cannot be used to build a message, and the lapse becomes a \replace{}, \skipm{} or \neglect{} mutation that an unmodified verifier analyses. Its published decision is a syntactic test on derivation hypotheses, which cannot represent a lost skill or a cue-prompted recall and gives no reason for its verdict.
We develop a reasoning framework for \forget{} in the ambiguity-blocking, team-defeat Defeasible Logic DL($\partial$). Per human transition, the cognitive state is compiled into a theory where possession and usability are separate, each derivation of a candidate message is a named conclusion, lapses of values, inference rules and derivations are defeaters, and cues are prioritised exceptions that overrule a lapse without removing it. Decisions are read from proof tags, and proofs are explanations.
We characterise what the compiled theories conclude and which proof-theoretic choices this depends on: recovery from several lapses requires team defeat, while ambiguity blocking and propagation agree. The framework conservatively extends and strictly separates the published semantics, its decisions stay sound under bounded search, and usability is decidable in polynomial time when no exact derivation is forgotten. The implementation in \XMen~2.0 agrees with an independent encoding of DL($\partial$), whose team-defeat defect the comparison exposed, and its decisions change Tamarin verdicts. A preliminary model of adversarial cues shows where conflicts become two-sided and the choice of semantics decides whether a human in doubt acts or withholds.
\keywords{Security ceremonies \and Mutations \and Defeasible logic \and Human error \and Tamarin}
\end{abstract}
